The Question

What can mycelial networks teach us about coordination between spacecraft, operators, and AI?

“Nothing between us is empty.”

Mycelial networks demonstrate a form of distributed organization in which individual components respond to local conditions while remaining connected through a larger network. This project explores whether those principles can inspire computational systems that coordinate without requiring a single centralized intelligence.

Mycelium → AI → Space

Each spacecraft is modeled as a local node. AI agents interpret information, identify patterns, and dynamically reinforce useful communication pathways. Human operators remain responsible for high-consequence decisions.

When a communication pathway fails, the network does not necessarily collapse. Instead, neighboring nodes can discover alternative routes, similar to how biological networks adapt to changing conditions.

Bioelectronic Interface

A future physical implementation could investigate organic electrochemical transistors and other bioelectronic interfaces as a bridge between biological signaling and computational systems.

The present demonstration does not claim to reproduce a biological transistor. Instead, it provides a computational model for exploring how adaptive signaling could influence distributed networks.

Earth + Space

The same architecture could theoretically support environmental monitoring and restoration. Distributed sensors could observe local ecological conditions while AI systems identify network-level patterns and human operators retain authority over consequential interventions.

The long-term vision is a technology architecture capable of adapting with its environment rather than treating the environment as a passive resource.

Ethical Questions

How do we ensure technologies inspired by living systems respect the systems they rely upon?

How do we prevent exploitation of biological resources, human labor, and ecological data?

Who controls increasingly autonomous infrastructure?

When should an AI system defer to human judgment?

And how can technological innovation be balanced with fairness, ecological sustainability, and human agency?
